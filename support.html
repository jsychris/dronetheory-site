Frequently asked questions

Is this an official CAA app?

No. Drone Theory is an independent study aid. It isn't made by or affiliated with the Civil Aviation Authority, and its questions are original — written from the Drone and Model Aircraft Code and public CAA guidance, not copied from the real test. For official rules, always check caa.co.uk and take the real Flyer ID test at register-drones.caa.co.uk.

How closely do the mock exams match the real Flyer ID test?

The Full Mock uses the real format: 40 multiple-choice questions with a pass mark of 30 (75%), untimed — the real test is free, online and open book. Quick Mock, Smart Practice and the Dynamic Exam let you practise in shorter sessions with the same 75% pass mark.

Does it cover the A2 CofC?

Yes. Go to Profile → Study mode and choose Flyer ID + A2 CofC. Advanced-operations questions — in the style of RAE theory papers — are then mixed into every exam. Flyer ID mode leaves them out. The real A2 CofC exam is taken through a CAA-approved Recognised Assessment Entity.

Does it cover the new 2026 rules?

Yes. The content is written against the rules in force from 1 January 2026: the 100 g Flyer ID threshold, UK class marks (UK0–UK6), Remote ID, and the green flashing light for night flying. Rules are still changing in stages, so always check caa.co.uk before you fly.

Do I need an internet connection or an account?

No to both. Everything works offline, there's no sign-up, and the app has no adverts or tracking.

Will I lose my progress if I change phone?

Your progress is stored only on your device, so it isn't synced between devices. It is kept in your normal iPhone or iPad backup, so restoring a backup to a new device should bring it with you.

How do I reset my progress?

Go to Profile → Reset all progress and confirm. This clears your XP, streaks, badges, completed lessons and exam history. It can't be undone.

How up to date is the content?

The content reflects the UK rules and Flyer ID test format as of 2026, including the January 2026 changes. Rules do change — a further Remote ID wave arrives on 1 January 2028 — so always check caa.co.uk and the Drone Code before you fly. If you think something is out of date, please open an issue.

Which devices are supported?

iPhone and iPad running iOS 17 or later.
